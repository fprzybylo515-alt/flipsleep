The science behind it

Stanford's dual-mode textile

In 2017, Yi Cui's and Shanhui Fan's groups at Stanford showed that one piece of fabric can do both passive radiative heating and cooling with no energy input. You switch modes by turning it inside out (Hsu et al., Science Advances 2017). FlipSleep takes that idea from clothing to bedding.

32–36°Cskin held in range through a 9°C swing in room temperature

How it works: a carbon/copper bilayer emitter sits inside infrared-transparent nanoporous polyethylene (nanoPE), the same base material as kitchen wrap. With the carbon side out, body heat radiates away. With the copper side out, radiation drops and heat stays in (Stanford Engineering). The emitter is also set at different distances from the skin on each side, which strengthens the effect (OSTI).
